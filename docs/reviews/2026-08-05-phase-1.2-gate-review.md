## 4. Findings

### Critical

**C2A-01 — Formal authority granted to two AI systems with no AI-use policy, no vendor assessment, and no independent human review.**

`GOV-REG-001` registers *Implementation Authority: OpenAI Codex* and *Independent Technical Reviewer: Claude AI (Advisory)*. The authority boundaries written for both roles are well-drafted — Codex "cannot ratify governance, approve its own work, broaden scope, release software, or override a denial", and the reviewer's findings are explicitly "not binding approval". That framing is correct and I endorse it.

What is missing is everything the Constitution requires around such a grant:

- **No AI-use policy exists** (Phase 1 finding H-22, now escalated by a formal authority grant). No rules on what repository or data content may be sent to either vendor, no provenance requirement for AI-authored artifacts, no evaluation, no audit-trail requirement, no disclosure.
- **No third-party/vendor risk review** for OpenAI or Anthropic, despite the Constitution applying to "every ... third-party relationship" and the Privacy by Design Standard requiring recipient, retention, training-terms, and contract analysis before any such relationship.
- **No independent human review anywhere in the chain.** The registry's only independent role is held by an AI whose output it declares non-binding. The effective count of independent human reviewers is zero.
- **Phase 2A is precisely where this bites.** Track A's first deliverable is the Governance Engine — the control that constrains AI. Having an AI implement it, reviewed by an AI, approved by the same human who authored the requirement, with no AI-use policy, is not a structure I can certify as sound.

*Required before Phase 2A:* write the internal AI-use policy; record a vendor-risk position for both providers; add a rule that AI-authored governance, security, and safety artifacts require documented line-by-line human review before ratification.

---

**C2A-02 — Separation of duties is unsatisfied, and the residual risk is not recorded.**

Constitutional Enforcement Rule 6: *"Authors cannot solely approve G2/G3 policy, scoring, AI, security, privacy, or release changes."* Ratifying the Constitution and eleven binding standards is unambiguously G3.

Every ratified artifact records `Owner: Andrew Penistone` and `Approver: Andrew Penistone`. The same person holds Constitutional Authority, Governance Owner, Release Authority, and Dispute Resolver.

There is a defensible reading in which the *author* is Codex and the *approver* is the human, technically satisfying the letter of Rule 6. I do not think that reading survives contact with the rule's intent, and in any case it produces a structure with no independent human check on any G3 decision — including the decision to disregard a finding.

Phase 1 finding H-20 asked for a minimum-viable governance design for a small team. Phase 1.2 has not solved it; it has **formalised the single-point-of-authority structure into the registry** without acknowledging it. Constitutional Security Principle 10 requires that *"material residual risks require explicit named human acceptance."* This risk is material, and no acceptance is recorded.

*Required before Phase 2A:* either name at least one independent human reviewer for G3 decisions (an external advisor or contracted reviewer is sufficient), or record an explicit, named, time-bound residual-risk acceptance in the registry under Security Principle 10, with the compensating controls stated.

---

**C2A-03 — No disposition recorded for the prior review's findings.**

The Phase 1 review produced 7 Critical, 26 High, 35 Medium and 11 Low findings and is committed to the repository. Ratification then proceeded. The registry's approval history lists "Claude AI (Advisory)" in the technical-review column for all three rows, but **records no disposition for any individual finding** — nothing marked remediated, accepted, deferred, or rejected, and no reference to the review document.

Five Criticals were left fully open and the artifacts containing them were ratified around them. That may well be the right call — but under the Constitution's own rule that *"absence of compliance evidence is treated as non-compliance,"* an unrecorded decision is not a decision.

*Required before Phase 2A:* add a findings-disposition record to the registry (or a linked register) marking each Critical and High as remediated / accepted-with-rationale / deferred-to-named-phase, with the Release Authority named against each.

### High

**H-01 — The CI enforcement layer is non-functional.**
`future-ci.yml` triggers only on `workflow_dispatch` and its sole job is gated `if: ${{ false }}` — it cannot execute under any condition. As a placeholder that makes no false claim, it is honest. As the constitutional enforcement layer required by Governance Enforcement Rule 7, it does not exist.

This matters now, not later, because **two of the Phase 1.2 exit-evidence claims are mechanically checkable with zero dependencies and were verified by hand instead**: link resolution, and absence of source/manifests/credentials/build output. A phase-boundary guard that fails the build when a `package.json`, a source file, or a credential pattern appears is perhaps thirty lines of YAML and directly enforces the constraint the whole phase rests on. Also absent: secret scanning, Dependabot, CodeQL — all GitHub-native, free, and dependency-free.

Minor supply-chain note within the disabled job: `actions/checkout@v4` is a mutable tag, not a pinned SHA, contrary to the lockfile/provenance requirements in `SEC-DES-001`.

**H-02 — Ratified artifacts do not satisfy the Constitution's own Governance Versioning clause, and use a status value outside the effective vocabulary.**
The Constitution requires each version to contain *"a version number, content digest, status, approval record, approvers, approved time, effective time, superseded version, change summary, compatibility statement, and required migrations."* The new headers supply version, status, approvers, effective time and (via the registry) an approval record. **Content digest, approved time, superseded version, change summary, compatibility statement, and required migrations are absent from every ratified artifact, including the Constitution itself.**

Separately, `ARCH-VER-001` — now Ratified and effective — defines the permitted status values as `draft`, `approved`, `active`, `retired`, `revoked`. Every ratified document uses `Ratified`, which is not among them. An effective binding standard is being violated by every artifact that carries it, including that standard.

The Versioning Framework also states that *"`not_applicable` is an explicit state with a reason; omission is not equivalent to not applicable."* Omitting the predecessor field on a `1.0.0` release is therefore non-conforming, not self-evident.

**H-03 — Ratified documents depend on unratified ones.**
Four substantive documents received no metadata block and remain formally unratified: `product-requirements.md`, `data-model.md`, `scoring-methodology.md`, `architecture-decisions.md`. Meanwhile `system-architecture.md` and `security-design.md` — which sit in the same directory, cite those documents, and depend on them — were ratified. The authority chain now runs from ratified artifacts down into unratified ones. Either the omission is deliberate (in which case record it, with the dependency inversion explicitly accepted) or it is an oversight in the ratification sweep.

**H-04 — `CODEOWNERS` is inert, and the governed process was not used to deliver Phase 1.2.**
`CODEOWNERS` has no effect without a branch-protection rule requiring code-owner review. I could not verify branch protection (no `gh` CLI in this environment) — it must be confirmed. Independently: both commits are direct commits to `main` with no merge commits and no PR references, so the PR template, its governance checklist, and the issue forms have never been exercised. A bootstrap commit is a reasonable exception; an *unrecorded* exception is not, given Enforcement Rule 11.

**H-05 — Operational role ownership is still unassigned.**
`GOV-OPS-001` (now Ratified) defines eight accountable roles: product owner, wellness safety reviewer, privacy lead, security lead, scoring owner, data steward, engineering owner, support/operations owner. The registry names none of them. Several hold stop-ship authority the Constitution treats as independent. Ratifying the specification that creates the roles, without filling them, leaves that authority unexercisable.

## 5. Assessment

The direction is right and the discipline is real. The phase boundary was held exactly — no code, no manifests, no credentials, no dependencies, and every one of the 139 documentation links resolves. The registry is a genuinely good document: the authority boundaries are precise, the AI roles are constrained in the right places, and the change-control section correctly forbids rewriting history. The issue forms and PR template encode the G0–G3 classification and the evidence requirement rather than gesturing at them. This is a competent phase.

The gap is that Phase 1.2 largely applied *metadata* to the problem my prior review identified, rather than closing it. Ratification headers were added, but not the six fields the Constitution itself demands. A registry was created, but it names one person in every seat and no human independent of the work. Two AI systems were granted formal roles in the governance chain — the correct instinct, expressed with the correct constraints — without the AI-use policy, vendor assessment, or human-review requirement that would make the grant safe. And the CI file, which is the one control that could mechanically enforce the phase boundary from here on, was written so that it cannot run.

None of this is hard to fix. The six items in §6 are hours to days of work, not weeks, and none require resolving the five open Criticals from Phase 1 — those correctly belong to the data-path track.

## 6. Decision: may the repository proceed to Phase 2A?

**Yes — conditionally.** Phase 2A as scoped (repository standards, CI policy checks, stack ADRs, governance registry and engine skeleton, synthetic data only, no personal-data schema frozen) may begin once the following six items are closed. They are ordered by dependency.

1. **Write the internal AI-use policy** and record a vendor-risk position for the Implementation Authority and Technical Reviewer roles. Include an explicit rule that AI-authored governance, security, and safety artifacts require documented human review before ratification. *(C2A-01)*
2. **Resolve independent review.** Name at least one independent human reviewer for G3 decisions, or record a named, time-bound residual-risk acceptance in the registry under Security Principle 10 with the compensating controls stated. *(C2A-02, H-05)*
3. **Record findings disposition.** Mark every Critical and High from the Phase 1 review as remediated / accepted / deferred, with the Release Authority named against each. *(C2A-03)*
4. **Complete the ratified-artifact metadata.** Add content digest, approved time, change summary, compatibility statement, and explicit `not_applicable` for predecessor. Reconcile `Status: Ratified` with `ARCH-VER-001`'s vocabulary — either amend the framework as a recorded G2 change or adopt `active`. Ratify or explicitly mark-as-draft the four omitted documents. *(H-02, H-03)*
5. **Make CI functional.** On pull request: documentation link check, phase-boundary guard (fail on source files, dependency manifests, credential patterns, or build output), and secret scanning. Enable Dependabot; pin actions to SHAs. This converts §1's manual verification into a standing control. *(H-01)*
6. **Bind identity and enforce the process.** Configure the git author name, enable commit signing, tag the ratified versions, confirm branch protection requires CODEOWNERS review, and record the Phase 1.2 direct-to-main delivery as an accepted exception. *(H-04, M-01–M-04)*

**Still blocked, unchanged from the prior review:** any Phase 2 work that defines, stores, or processes a personal-data path — data model freeze, migrations, ingest, check-in, scoring implementation, client development — remains gated on Phase 1 findings C-03 through C-07 plus H-01, H-02, H-03, H-05, H-06, H-07 and H-09 from that report.

**Recommended sequencing note.** Items 4, 5 and 6 are natural first commits *of* Phase 2A rather than prerequisites to it, and I would not object to them being delivered that way provided items 1, 2 and 3 land first. Items 1–3 are genuine prerequisites: they govern how Phase 2A's own work gets authored, reviewed and approved, and Phase 2A's first deliverable is the Governance Engine itself.
