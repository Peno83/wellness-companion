## 3. Assessment of the three new governance documents

**`GOV-AI-001` (AI Use Policy) — strong.** Better than most commercial internal AI policies I have reviewed. Specifically good:

- Rule 4 — *"AI must never approve its own output, close its own finding, accept residual risk, or satisfy a requirement for independent human approval."* This is the correct load-bearing sentence and it is stated without hedging.
- Prompt governance treats *"repository content, tool output, retrieved text, and user-controlled content"* as untrusted instructions. That is prompt-injection awareness applied to the internal toolchain, not just the future product boundary, and it is the right generalisation.
- *"Redaction must be verified, not assumed."*
- Model-update rules prohibit floating aliases and automatic adoption for governed workflows.
- Rule 6 keeps a change **blocked** when separation of duties cannot be met, rather than inventing an exception.

**`GOV-RISK-001` (Residual Risk Register) — strong, and notably honest.** R-001's rationale — *"central accountability is clearer than fictitious independence at this stage"* — is the right call, stated plainly. The acceptance conditions do not overclaim: *"R-001 through R-004 do not satisfy constitutional separation of duties for G2/G3 changes."* Every row is time-bounded, and expiry converts acceptance back to open rather than lapsing silently. This is how a residual-risk register should read.

**`GOV-FIND-001` (Review Findings Register) — sound structure, imperfect first execution.** Controlled decision and status vocabularies, evidence column, target-phase column, and the rule that *"AI advice alone cannot close a finding."* All 7 Phase 1 Criticals and 25 of 26 Phase 1 Highs are registered with dispositions. Three execution defects are recorded at H-03, H-04 and H-01 below.

## 4. Findings

### Critical

**C-01 — The Constitution was substantively amended in place at an unchanged version, and eleven ratified artifacts were mutated without version bumps.**

The Constitution gained a new normative paragraph (subordinating internal AI use, findings disposition and risk acceptance to itself) and three new entries in *Related binding standards*. Version remains `1.0.0`. Effective Date remains `2026-08-05`. No change-log entry exists.

Separately, eleven ratified artifacts had their `Owner` and `Approver` changed from `Andrew Penfold` to `Andrew Penistone` in place — including the Governance Registry's **Approval History and Change Log rows**.

This breaches four rules the project ratified the previous day:

- Constitution, *Governance Versioning*: *"Each version is immutable."* Adding rules is a **Minor** change requiring constitutional approval and compatibility assessment — i.e. `1.1.0`, not an edit to `1.0.0`.
- Constitution, *Change Approval Process* step 6: *"Publish: Create an immutable version, digest, effective date, change log, and accessible summary."*
- `ARCH-VER-001`: *"Never mutate a released artifact in place; publish a new version."*
- `GOV-REG-001`, *Change control*: role changes require *"a new immutable registry version and change-log entry"*, and *"historical approval and change-log entries must not be rewritten or removed."*

I accept that correcting a misspelled surname is editorially trivial and was plainly right to do. That is not the issue. The issue is that **the first real exercise of the change-control process bypassed it**, on the artifacts that define it. If in-place mutation of ratified governance is acceptable when the change feels small, the immutability guarantee is advisory — and Phase 2A is where that guarantee starts being enforced in code.

*Fix:* publish Constitution `1.1.0` with change summary, compatibility statement and effective date (or revert the amendment and re-issue properly); publish `1.0.1` for the name-corrected artifacts with a change-log line each; restore the Registry's historical rows and record the correction as a new entry rather than a rewrite.

---

**C-02 — The entire Phase 1.3 governance layer is uncommitted.**

`git log` shows two commits, both from Phase 1.2. Every Phase 1.3 artifact — the AI policy, both registers, the CI workflow, the Constitution amendment, the eleven corrections, and both independent review reports — exists only in an untracked or unstaged working tree.

Consequences: no immutable record, no content digest, no author attribution, no timestamp, nothing pushed to the remote, and no way for any reviewer other than someone at this machine to verify what was ratified. A governance layer that is not in version control is not evidence; under the Constitution's own rule that *"absence of compliance evidence is treated as non-compliance,"* Phase 1.3 is not yet delivered.

This is minutes of work, but it is genuinely gate-blocking: approving a phase whose artifacts are not in the record would be approving something unverifiable.

### High

**H-01 — The documentation CI is guaranteed to fail on its first run, has never been executed, and the finding it closes was closed on that evidence.**

`markdownlint-cli2-action` runs with **no configuration file** (`.markdownlint*` / `.markdownlint-cli2*` absent), so default rules apply — including MD013 at an 80-character limit. I measured the repository: **1,294 lines exceed 80 characters**, concentrated in exactly the governance documents this workflow is meant to protect (100 in the Constitution alone). `cspell-action` likewise runs with no dictionary (`cspell.json` absent) against a corpus dense with domain vocabulary — *pseudonymous, rehydration, subprocessors, tombstoned, WHOOP, DSAR, Penistone*.

The workflow will go red immediately. `GOV-FIND-001` marks P1.2-H-01 **Closed** with this workflow as evidence, while the register's own rule states *"`Closed` requires linked evidence and human confirmation."* An uncommitted, never-executed, predictably failing workflow is not confirmation.

*Fix:* add `.markdownlint-cli2.jsonc` and `cspell.json` with a project dictionary, run it, and only close P1.2-H-01 once it is green on a real run.

**H-02 — There is no phase-boundary guard, and the workflow cannot see non-Markdown changes.**

Both triggers filter on `paths: ["**/*.md", ".github/workflows/documentation-quality.yml"]`. A pull request adding `package.json`, `src/app.ts`, a `Dockerfile`, or a credential file **triggers no workflow at all**.

The single most valuable check available in a documentation-only phase — fail the build if application source, a dependency manifest, build output, or a credential pattern appears — is absent, and the path filter would exclude it from inspection even if present. I verified the boundary by hand for this review, as I did for Phase 1.2; that manual verification is exactly what should now be mechanised, and it is roughly thirty lines of YAML with no dependencies.

**H-03 — Phase 1 finding H-13 was dropped from traceability on an incorrect premise.**

`GOV-FIND-001` states: *"The source review contains no H-13."* It does. H-13 — *the safety interruption has no explainability contract* — appears five times in the source review: in the body of Critical C-05, in missing-requirement MR-10, in the per-document assessment of `explainability-standard.md`, as prioritised recommendation #17, and in the Phase 1.2 closure-scope table.

In fairness, my own report is the proximate cause: H-13 was folded into C-05 item 3 and cross-referenced rather than given its own heading, so a heading-based sweep would miss it. But the conclusion drawn was that the finding does not exist, and the consequence is that **the explainability gap on the product's highest-stakes user-facing output — the crisis/safety interruption — now has no owner, no target phase, and no disposition anywhere.**

*Fix:* register P1.1-H-13 with target phase "before health features", alongside C-05.

**H-04 — Thirteen Phase 1.2 findings are unregistered with no deferral statement.**

The register captures the Phase 1.2 Criticals and Highs but omits all 9 Medium and 4 Low findings. Unlike the Phase 1 Mediums and Lows — which are explicitly deferred to the source document with a stated migration obligation — these have no statement at all. Several are live and unaddressed: unsigned commits (M-01), placeholder author identity (M-02), no version tags (M-04), missing `SECURITY.md`/`LICENSE`/`CONTRIBUTING.md` (M-05), and the observation that the Phase 1.2 exit gate as written was not met (M-09).

**H-05 — Four unpinned third-party actions contradict two ratified documents.**

`actions/checkout@v4`, `DavidAnson/markdownlint-cli2-action@v20`, `lycheeverse/lychee-action@v2`, `streetsidesoftware/cspell-action@v7` are all mutable major tags. `GOV-AI-001` §Model updates — ratified in this same phase — states *"floating aliases must not be treated as immutable versions."* `SEC-DES-001` requires locked dependencies, artifact provenance and isolated CI. Three of the four are third-party actions with write access to the workflow runtime of a governance repository. No `dependabot.yml` exists to track them.

**H-06 — Three gaps in the AI governance implementation.**

1. **Providers were grandfathered.** §Vendor risk requires a thirteen-point assessment *"before a new provider or materially different service mode is approved."* OpenAI Codex and Anthropic Claude are already listed as approved in the same document, with no such assessment recorded. R-006 accepts vendor dependency in general, which is not the same as having performed the assessment.
2. **No AI-interaction record exists.** §Prompt governance requires that inputs and outputs used for a decision retain *"provider, model identifier, prompt-template version where applicable, time, operator, purpose, and artifact references."* Phases 1.1 through 1.3 were substantially AI-assisted and no such record exists for any artifact.
3. **No prompt templates are source-controlled**, though §Prompt governance requires reusable templates to be versioned under `ARCH-VER-001`.

Credit where due: using *different* providers for authoring and review is a genuine control against correlated error, and R-004 names the risk correctly. That control should be stated explicitly in the policy rather than left as an accident of the current arrangement.

**H-07 — The four Phase 1.2 Highs I gated on Phase 2A remain open.**

P1.2-H-02 (metadata non-conformance and status vocabulary), H-03 (ratified documents depending on unratified ones), H-04 (CODEOWNERS not operational), H-05 (operational roles unassigned) are all registered honestly as **Open**. They were the "acceptable as Phase 2A's first commits" set in my prior review and I hold that position — but they are now tracked rather than closed, and H-02's vocabulary conflict has propagated to three additional documents.

## 6. On solo-founder proportionality

The design is proportionate and, more importantly, honest. It does not manufacture independence it does not have; it blocks G3 rather than fudging it; it uses AI for breadth while explicitly denying it authority; and every acceptance expires. For a solo founder building a health-adjacent product, this is the right shape.

The risk has now inverted. It is no longer that governance is too thin — it is that **upkeep capacity is the binding constraint**. Fifteen documents, four registers, eight unassigned roles, quarterly review obligations on every artifact, and a change-control process that was bypassed on its first real use, all maintained by one person who is also the implementer, approver and releaser.

The failure mode to watch for is not a governance gap. It is governance drift: registers that stop being updated, expiry dates that pass unreviewed, and in-place edits that feel too small to version. C-01 is the first instance, three days in.

My recommendation for the next phase is therefore **consolidation and automation, not expansion**: no new governance documents unless one is retired or merged; move the mechanically checkable rules (metadata completeness, status vocabulary, expiry dates, register-to-review coverage) into CI so the machine holds the process rather than the person; and set a calendar reminder for the 2026-11-05 review dates now, because nothing else will surface them.
